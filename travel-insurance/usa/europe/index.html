What to check before buying a Europe policy

Travel insurance is sold under rules and eligibility conditions tied to where you live. A policy marketed to another country may not be available to a U.S. resident, so residence eligibility should be the first filter in any comparison.

Emergency medicalCompare the medical limit, deductible, exclusions and whether the provider can pay hospitals directly.
Medical evacuationCheck who decides that transport is medically necessary and where the policy can send you.
CancellationUse the real eligible non-refundable cost, not the total value of everything you plan to spend.
Interruption & delayRead waiting periods and how independently booked flights, trains, ferries or tours are treated.

Medicare and Europe: the point many older travelers miss

Medicare states that it usually does not cover health care while you are traveling outside the United States, with limited exceptions. Medicare Advantage plans may offer additional benefits and some Medigap policies can include foreign travel emergency benefits. That means “I have Medicare” is not enough information to decide whether you need extra medical protection for Europe.

Before buying anything, check your exact plan documents and ask how overseas emergency treatment, urgent care, evacuation and reimbursement work. Then compare only the gaps you still have.

When cancellation cover becomes more important

A flexible city break with refundable hotels is different from a €10,000 cruise, escorted tour or family trip with prepaid rail and apartment bookings. Focus on the amount you would actually lose if a covered event forces you to cancel.

  • Check the definition of a covered reason.
  • Check purchase deadlines for any pre-existing-condition waiver or optional benefits.
  • Understand whether airline credits reduce the amount that can be claimed.
  • Keep invoices, booking confirmations and cancellation terms.

How to compare travel insurance for Europe from the USA

Start by filtering for policies that can legally be bought by residents of your U.S. state. Next, enter the dates, destinations and ages of everyone traveling. Do not select a plan by headline price alone: the limits and exclusions decide what it can pay.

  1. Medical emergencies: compare the benefit limit, deductible, how claims are paid and 24-hour assistance.
  2. Emergency evacuation: check who authorizes transport and whether it is to an appropriate hospital or back to the United States.
  3. Cancellation and interruption: total up eligible non-refundable bookings and read the list of covered reasons, waiting periods and purchase deadlines.
  4. Pre-existing conditions: check exclusions, disclosures, stability rules and any waiver conditions.
  5. Trip activities: verify skiing, hiking, cruises, cycling and other planned activities are covered.

Use the travel insurance comparison form to organize your trip details. Then verify actual eligibility and policy wording with the insurer.

Travel medical insurance vs trip cancellation: what's different?

Emergency medicalHelps pay for covered illness or injury, subject to limits, deductibles and exclusions.
Medical evacuationCan arrange necessary medical transport under stated conditions; it does not promise a flight home on request.
CancellationMay reimburse eligible prepaid, non-refundable costs before the trip if a specified covered reason occurs.
Interruption and delaysCan address covered events after departure; missed separate flights or trains are not automatically covered.
BaggageCheck per-item limits, theft exclusions, proof-of-ownership rules and coverage for delayed bags.

The U.S. Department of State recommends reviewing international medical and evacuation protection before departure.

Do U.S. citizens need Schengen travel insurance?

For ordinary short visa-free tourism by U.S. passport holders, a Schengen visa insurance certificate is generally not a standard entry requirement. Travel medical protection can still be important because medical bills and evacuation may be expensive. Someone living in the U.S. whose nationality requires a Schengen visa must instead follow the visa insurance requirements, including the €30,000 minimum and relevant territory rules.

U.S.-to-Europe insurance checklist before buying

  • The insurer accepts residents of your state.
  • Coverage dates include travel and all country stops, not just Schengen countries.
  • Medical, evacuation and assistance limits are clear.
  • Pre-existing conditions and activities are addressed in writing.
  • Cancellation amounts reflect eligible non-refundable bookings.
  • Claims, direct payment or reimbursement rules are understood.
  • The policy number and emergency contact are saved offline.

Frequently asked questions

Does Medicare cover medical care in Europe?

Original Medicare generally does not cover health care outside the U.S. except in limited circumstances. Some Medicare Advantage and Medigap arrangements may differ, so verify your own plan.

Is travel insurance required for U.S. tourists visiting Europe?

For ordinary short visa-free tourism, it is generally a risk-management choice rather than a standard Schengen entry requirement for U.S. citizens. If your personal nationality or visa status requires a Schengen visa, use the visa-insurance rules instead.

How much medical coverage should I buy?

There is no single number that fits every traveler. Compare your existing health coverage, destination, age, trip length, provider network and the cost of medical evacuation. The policy wording matters as much as the headline limit.

Does my U.S. health insurance work in Europe?

Some private plans provide overseas benefits, but rules vary. Check directly with your provider. Original Medicare generally does not cover most care outside the United States, except in limited cases.

Can credit-card coverage replace travel medical insurance?

Not always. Card benefits can focus on trip delay, baggage or cancellation and may have no international medical cover. Read the certificate of insurance before relying on it.
