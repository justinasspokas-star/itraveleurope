Quick answer for U.S. travelers

Before buying a Europe policy, check what health protection you already have and where it stops. Many U.S. health arrangements have limited overseas networks, Medicare usually has very limited coverage outside the United States, and travel insurance can add emergency medical, evacuation, cancellation, interruption and delay benefits that domestic coverage may not provide.

Does U.S. health insurance cover you in Europe?

There is no universal answer. Employer plans, marketplace plans, retiree coverage and private insurers can treat overseas care differently. Ask your insurer whether emergency care outside the United States is covered, whether you must pay first and claim reimbursement, whether there is an overseas network and whether medical evacuation is included. Get the answer in writing when possible.

Medicare, Medicare Advantage and Medigap

Original Medicare generally does not cover healthcare outside the United States except in limited circumstances. Medicare Advantage plans can have plan-specific emergency or urgent-care benefits abroad, while some Medigap policies include limited foreign travel emergency benefits subject to their own conditions. None of those labels should be treated as a substitute for checking the exact plan. Our Medicare and Europe guide explains the differences in detail.

Credit-card travel insurance can help—but check how it is activated

Premium credit cards may include trip delay, baggage, rental-car or cancellation benefits, but activation rules can depend on how much of the trip was paid with the card. Medical and evacuation coverage may be absent or limited. Treat card benefits as one layer of protection and compare them against the gaps in your actual itinerary.

How much emergency medical cover do you need?

There is no single correct dollar amount for every traveler. Instead, compare the medical maximum with the deductible, exclusions, pre-existing-condition wording, assistance process and destination. An older traveler with complex health needs has a different risk profile from a healthy 25-year-old taking a five-day city break.

Medical evacuation is not the same as a flight home

Evacuation benefits typically focus on medically necessary transport arranged or approved by the assistance provider. That could mean transport to an appropriate hospital, not automatically repatriation to the United States. This matters for cruises, Greek islands, ski areas and rural trips.

Cancellation cover matters when the trip cost is high

European vacations can involve substantial prepaid costs: transatlantic flights, villas, cruises, guided tours, rail passes and non-refundable hotels. Compare cancellation limits against the eligible money you would actually lose. “Cancel for any reason” is a separate optional benefit on some policies and should not be confused with standard cancellation coverage.

Pre-existing conditions: timing can matter

Some policies offer a waiver of certain pre-existing-condition exclusions when purchase and other eligibility rules are met. Read the definition carefully and pay attention to purchase deadlines after the initial trip deposit. A stable condition, a recent medication change and an ongoing diagnostic investigation may be treated differently.

Example: three-country Europe trip

For a two-week route from New York to Madrid, Paris and Rome, check that the policy covers the full date range and territory, then review independently booked trains and flights. If a delayed flight makes you miss a separately booked train or hotel night, coverage depends on the policy's delay and missed-connection wording—not simply on the fact that you bought travel insurance.

U.S. traveler checklist before buying

  1. Ask your U.S. health insurer what overseas emergency care is covered.
  2. If you have Medicare, Medicare Advantage or Medigap, verify the exact foreign-travel benefit.
  3. Check credit-card benefits before buying duplicate protection.
  4. Calculate eligible non-refundable trip cost.
  5. Compare medical and evacuation benefits separately.
  6. Review pre-existing-condition rules and purchase deadlines.
  7. Check all European destinations and activities.
  8. Save assistance numbers and policy documents before departure.

What to check before buying a Europe policy

Travel insurance is sold under rules and eligibility conditions tied to where you live. A policy marketed to another country may not be available to a U.S. resident, so residence eligibility should be the first filter in any comparison.

Emergency medicalCompare the medical limit, deductible, exclusions and whether the provider can pay hospitals directly.
Medical evacuationCheck who decides that transport is medically necessary and where the policy can send you.
CancellationUse the real eligible non-refundable cost, not the total value of everything you plan to spend.
Interruption & delayRead waiting periods and how independently booked flights, trains, ferries or tours are treated.

Medicare and Europe: the point many older travelers miss

Medicare states that it usually does not cover health care while you are traveling outside the United States, with limited exceptions. Medicare Advantage plans may offer additional benefits and some Medigap policies can include foreign travel emergency benefits. That means “I have Medicare” is not enough information to decide whether you need extra medical protection for Europe.

Before buying anything, check your exact plan documents and ask how overseas emergency treatment, urgent care, evacuation and reimbursement work. Then compare only the gaps you still have.

When cancellation cover becomes more important

A flexible city break with refundable hotels is different from a €10,000 cruise, escorted tour or family trip with prepaid rail and apartment bookings. Focus on the amount you would actually lose if a covered event forces you to cancel.

  • Check the definition of a covered reason.
  • Check purchase deadlines for any pre-existing-condition waiver or optional benefits.
  • Understand whether airline credits reduce the amount that can be claimed.
  • Keep invoices, booking confirmations and cancellation terms.

Frequently asked questions

Does Medicare cover medical care in Europe?

Original Medicare generally does not cover health care outside the U.S. except in limited circumstances. Some Medicare Advantage and Medigap arrangements may differ, so verify your own plan.

Is travel insurance required for U.S. tourists visiting Europe?

For ordinary short visa-free tourism, it is generally a risk-management choice rather than a standard Schengen entry requirement for U.S. citizens. If your personal nationality or visa status requires a Schengen visa, use the visa-insurance rules instead.

How much medical coverage should I buy?

There is no single number that fits every traveler. Compare your existing health coverage, destination, age, trip length, provider network and the cost of medical evacuation. The policy wording matters as much as the headline limit.
