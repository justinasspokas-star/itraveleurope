Greece-specific planning: islands, ferries and medical transport

Greece is one of the clearest examples of why destination-specific planning matters. An Athens-only trip is relatively straightforward, but an island itinerary can depend on ferries, domestic flights and seasonal schedules. Check how your policy treats delays or cancellations involving ferries, and whether a missed onward booking is covered when the reservations were purchased separately.

Medical logistics can also be different on smaller islands. Emergency care may require transfer to a larger facility, so the medical-evacuation benefit deserves attention alongside the ordinary medical-expense limit. Read who must authorize transport and whether the insurer decides the appropriate facility. This is different from choosing to return home early.

If your plans include sailing, quad bikes, scooters, diving or hiking, review activity and vehicle exclusions carefully. A policy can cover a destination without covering every activity done there. Keep ferry, airline and accommodation confirmations because island disruption claims often depend on proving the original schedule and the extra expenses actually incurred.

Policy wording still controls the claim

Travel insurance only covers events, costs and circumstances defined by the policy. Buy for the wording you need, disclose information accurately and keep documentation for every significant booking.

Frequently asked questions

Does travel insurance cover a cancelled ferry?

It can cover certain resulting expenses when the cause and delay meet the policy terms. Read waiting periods and keep the operator’s written notice.

Is medical evacuation important for Greek islands?

It deserves attention because some treatment may require transport to a larger facility. Coverage must be arranged or approved under the policy rules.

Are scooters covered?

Medical or liability claims can be excluded if licensing, helmet, engine-size or local-law conditions are not met.
