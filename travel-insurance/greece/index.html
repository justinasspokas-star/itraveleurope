Quick answer: what matters most for a Greece trip?

For U.S. travelers, the biggest Greece-specific issues are usually medical treatment and evacuation, ferry or domestic-flight disruption, missed connections between separate bookings, and activity exclusions. A simple Athens city break and a two-week island-hopping itinerary can need very different protection, so compare the policy against the trip you have actually booked.

What to compare for travel insurance to Greece

Emergency medical

Check the medical limit, excess or deductible, assistance requirements and whether the policy can coordinate payment with the treating facility.

Medical evacuation

For island trips, read how transport to a larger medical facility is authorised and whether the insurer must arrange it in advance.

Ferry and flight disruption

Compare common-carrier delay, cancellation and interruption wording, including waiting periods and eligible extra expenses.

Activities and vehicles

Scooters, ATVs, sailing, diving and higher-risk excursions can have licensing, safety-equipment or activity-level conditions.

Greek island hopping changes the insurance risk

A Greece trip often combines an international flight with ferries, domestic flights and separately booked hotels. That creates more points where one delay can affect the next reservation. If you are travelling Athens → Santorini → Naxos → Mykonos, for example, check what the policy says about missed connections, travel delay and trip interruption when the bookings are independent.

Do not assume that every later loss is covered because the first ferry was delayed. Policies usually define the covered cause, minimum delay, eligible transport provider and the expenses they will reimburse. Build sensible connection time into the itinerary as well as buying insurance.

Ferry cancellations and delays: what to check before island hopping

Ferries are part of the travel plan for many Greek islands, but insurance treatment can vary. Before buying, look for the definitions of common carrier, travel delay, missed departure and trip interruption. Check how long a delay must last before a benefit begins and whether replacement transport, an extra hotel night or meals can qualify.

  • Keep ferry confirmations and boarding information.
  • If a sailing is cancelled or heavily delayed, obtain written confirmation from the operator when possible.
  • Keep receipts for replacement travel, meals and accommodation.
  • Check whether the policy requires you to contact the assistance team before making expensive alternative arrangements.

A policy does not replace good scheduling. If an international flight home is important, leaving a buffer between an island ferry and the flight can reduce a risk that insurance may not fully solve.

Medical treatment and evacuation from Greek islands

Medical access can differ between Athens, larger islands and smaller island destinations. For that reason, the headline emergency-medical limit is only one part of the comparison. Also read the assistance and evacuation terms.

Ask practical questions: who decides whether transport is medically necessary, must the insurer arrange it, where can the patient be moved, and what happens if a companion or child is travelling with the patient? These details can matter more on an island itinerary than on a short city break.

Your U.S. health plan may offer some overseas benefits, or none that are useful for your trip. Do not assume domestic health insurance will pay a Greek provider directly or arrange transport between islands. If you have an existing condition, medication or recent treatment, also review our pre-existing medical conditions guide.

Scooters, mopeds and ATVs: read the exclusions before renting

Renting a scooter or ATV is common on some islands, but travel-insurance coverage should never be assumed. Policies can restrict claims when the rider does not meet local licensing rules, the vehicle exceeds a permitted engine size, required protective equipment is not used, or the activity itself is excluded.

The rental company's damage or liability terms are also separate from your travel medical insurance. Before renting, check the licence requirements, helmet rules, rental agreement and your policy wording. A medical policy paying for an eligible injury does not necessarily mean damage to the rented vehicle is insured.

Diving, sailing, hiking and active excursions

Greece is not only beaches and city sightseeing. Diving, sailing, sea kayaking, hiking and organised excursions may be subject to activity definitions. A policy can distinguish between recreational and competitive activity, guided and unguided activity, diving depth, certification, altitude or the distance a boat travels from shore.

If an activity is a major reason for the trip, search the policy document for the activity name before buying. If the wording is unclear, confirm it with the provider rather than relying on a general label such as “sports covered.”

Match the policy to your Greece itinerary

Athens city break

Focus on emergency medical, cancellation, baggage and delay. If most bookings are flexible and the trip is short, you may need less cancellation protection than a complex island itinerary.

Athens + Santorini

Compare ferry or domestic-flight disruption, medical evacuation, missed connections and the prepaid value of hotels on both parts of the trip.

Cyclades island hopping

Prioritise interruption, multiple non-refundable bookings, assistance and transport coordination. Check every island and every activity is inside the policy territory and terms.

Crete road trip

Travel insurance and rental-car cover solve different problems. Compare medical and trip protection separately from collision damage, roadside assistance and liability in the rental contract.

Sailing or diving holiday

Check activity definitions, certification requirements, rescue or evacuation and any exclusions that apply offshore or below a stated diving depth.

Cancellation and interruption: insure the money you could actually lose

A Greece itinerary can contain several prepaid components: transatlantic flights, domestic flights, ferries, hotels, villas, tours and rental cars. Add up the non-refundable amount you would lose if a covered event prevented the trip or forced you to return home early.

Then compare that amount with the cancellation and interruption limits. A policy with a low premium is not automatically good value if its cancellation limit is far below your prepaid trip cost. Also check covered reasons and purchase deadlines; insurance normally does not cover every reason for changing your mind.

Baggage delay can matter more when you leave Athens quickly

If you fly into Athens and connect soon afterwards to an island, delayed baggage can become logistically harder to recover. Check the waiting period before baggage-delay benefits begin, the allowance for essential purchases and the documents required from the airline.

Keep receipts and the airline's baggage report. Valuable electronics, jewellery, sports equipment and other high-value items can have separate per-item limits that are much lower than the overall baggage limit.

Greece, Schengen and travel insurance

Greece is part of the Schengen Area, but entry rules and travel-insurance coverage are separate questions. Your nationality, length of stay and purpose of travel determine the applicable entry requirements, while the insurance policy determines what events and expenses are covered.

If your Europe trip includes Greece plus other Schengen countries, confirm that the policy territory includes the full itinerary and that your dates cover every travel day. For a broader starting point, see our travel insurance for Europe from the USA guide.

USA → Greece travel insurance checklist

  1. Confirm Greece and every other country on the itinerary are inside the policy territory.
  2. Compare emergency medical cover and medical evacuation separately.
  3. Check ferry, domestic-flight, missed-connection and interruption wording if island hopping.
  4. Add up non-refundable flights, hotels, ferries and tours before selecting cancellation cover.
  5. Review scooter, ATV, diving, sailing and hiking exclusions before booking those activities.
  6. Check any pre-existing-condition disclosure or screening requirements.
  7. Read baggage-delay and per-item limits if you are carrying expensive equipment.
  8. Save the insurer's emergency-assistance number and policy details offline.
  9. Keep booking confirmations, delay notices, medical records and receipts if something goes wrong.

Frequently asked questions

Does U.S. health insurance cover medical care in Greece?

Coverage outside the United States varies by health plan. Do not assume your domestic plan will pay foreign hospitals directly or cover medical evacuation. Check your existing benefits and the travel-insurance policy before departure.

Does travel insurance cover a cancelled ferry in Greece?

It can cover certain additional costs when the ferry disruption meets the policy's covered-reason, delay and common-carrier rules. Keep the operator's cancellation or delay notice and receipts for any eligible replacement transport or accommodation.

Is medical evacuation important for the Greek islands?

It can be an important comparison point because an illness or injury on a smaller island may require transport to a larger medical facility. Check how evacuation is authorised, arranged and paid under the policy.

Are scooters, mopeds or ATVs covered by travel insurance in Greece?

Not automatically. Medical or liability claims can be restricted when licensing, helmet, engine-size, rental-contract or local-law requirements are not met. Check the exact activity and vehicle wording before renting.

Do I need special cover for diving, sailing or other activities in Greece?

Possibly. Policies can set limits for diving depth, certification, organised excursions, sailing area, hiking altitude and other activities. Confirm that the exact activity you plan is included rather than assuming standard cover applies.

What should I insure on a Greek island-hopping trip?

Start with emergency medical and evacuation, then compare cancellation and interruption against your non-refundable flights, ferries and hotels. Also check missed-connection, baggage-delay and activity wording if your itinerary depends on several separate bookings.
