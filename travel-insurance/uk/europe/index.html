Quick answer for UK travelers

Use a valid GHIC or eligible EHIC where it applies, but compare travel insurance separately for medical repatriation, private healthcare, cancellation, baggage, delays and other policy benefits. GHIC/EHIC and travel insurance solve different problems.

What GHIC or EHIC can do

A UK GHIC or valid EHIC can provide access to medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries on the terms that apply locally. It is not a private medical-insurance policy and should not be treated as a replacement for broader travel insurance. Our GHIC vs travel insurance guide explains the distinction in detail.

Why repatriation matters

GHIC/EHIC does not provide medical repatriation back to the UK. A travel policy may cover medically necessary transport or repatriation subject to its own authorization and terms. That can be especially important on islands, ski trips, cruises or when specialist treatment is needed away from home.

Private healthcare and assistance

GHIC/EHIC is built around eligible state healthcare. If a traveler is taken to a private clinic or chooses private treatment, the card may not apply. Travel-insurance assistance can help identify suitable care and explain whether the insurer can arrange payment or whether the traveler should expect reimbursement.

Single-trip vs annual European insurance

Frequent travelers may compare annual multi-trip policies with single-trip cover. Check the maximum duration of each individual trip, age limits, winter-sports or cruise add-ons, geographic definitions and whether a longer holiday needs an extension.

Pre-existing medical conditions

Having a GHIC does not remove the need to disclose medical information when an insurer asks for it. Read the policy's medical screening, stability or declaration rules and check whether a change in treatment before departure affects cover.

UK traveler checklist

  1. Check that your GHIC/EHIC is valid for the destinations on your route.
  2. Understand which healthcare system the card gives access to.
  3. Compare medical repatriation and private-treatment wording.
  4. Review cancellation and interruption benefits for prepaid costs.
  5. Check annual-policy maximum trip duration if you travel often.
  6. Declare medical conditions accurately when required.
  7. Check winter sports, cruises or other activities separately.

Start with GHIC or EHIC—then look for the gaps

A UK Global Health Insurance Card (GHIC), or a valid UK-issued EHIC where it remains eligible, can help with medically necessary state healthcare during temporary stays in covered European countries. Access follows the rules of the country you are visiting, so treatment may not always be free.

That healthcare access is useful, but it is not the same product as travel insurance. It does not replace cover for risks such as medical repatriation to the UK, private treatment, cancellation, baggage or many transport disruptions.

Single-trip or annual European cover?

If you expect only one holiday, a single-trip policy can be the simpler comparison. Frequent travelers may prefer an annual policy, but the annual label does not guarantee every trip fits. Check the maximum duration per trip, destination region, age limits and activity restrictions.

  • Compare the maximum length of each trip.
  • Check whether all European destinations on your itinerary are included.
  • Read age limits and pre-existing-condition wording.
  • Do not assume winter sports or cruise cover is automatic.

Frequently asked questions

Is GHIC the same as travel insurance?

No. GHIC can help with eligible state healthcare in covered countries, but it does not replace travel insurance for repatriation, cancellation, baggage and other travel risks.

Does GHIC cover private hospitals?

GHIC is designed around state-provided healthcare under the local system. Do not assume private care is covered; check the rules for your destination and your travel-insurance policy.

Should I buy annual Europe travel insurance?

It can suit frequent travelers, but check maximum trip length, age limits, destination definitions, activities and pre-existing-condition rules before comparing it with single-trip cover.
