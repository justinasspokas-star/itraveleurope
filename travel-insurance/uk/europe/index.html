Quick answer: do UK travellers need travel insurance for Europe?

A GHIC or eligible EHIC can help with medically necessary state healthcare in participating European countries, but it is not a substitute for travel insurance. A separate policy can protect against risks such as medical repatriation, cancellation, interruption, baggage loss, delays, private treatment and activities, depending on the policy. For most UK travellers, the useful comparison is not simply “insured or uninsured” but which risks are already covered and which still need protection.

GHIC or EHIC vs travel insurance: start with the healthcare you already have

A UK Global Health Insurance Card (GHIC), or a valid UK-issued EHIC where it remains eligible, can help UK residents access medically necessary state healthcare during temporary stays in participating countries. Treatment is provided under the rules of the country you are visiting, so it may not always be free.

Travel insurance does a different job. It can include protection for costs that a GHIC or EHIC does not address, such as medical repatriation to the UK, cancellation, baggage and some private medical treatment. This distinction is especially important when comparing travel insurance UK to Europe because the cheapest policy is not automatically the policy with the most useful medical protection.

What does “best travel insurance for Europe” actually mean?

There is no single policy that is automatically best for every UK traveller. A weekend city break, a family holiday, a ski trip and a six-week multi-country itinerary expose you to different financial risks. Instead of looking for one universal “best travel insurance for Europe” winner, compare the parts of the policy that match your trip.

  1. Medical and repatriation: enough cover for the destinations and activities on your itinerary.
  2. Cancellation: aligned with the prepaid, non-refundable amount you could actually lose.
  3. Excess: the amount you may need to pay towards an eligible claim.
  4. Baggage and valuables: check both total and per-item limits.
  5. Delays and missed departures: especially important for separate flights, ferries and rail connections.
  6. Activities: winter sports, cruises, cycling and higher-risk activities may need specific cover.
  7. Medical history: make sure the insurer has the information it asks for and that the resulting terms are clear.

Use your actual trip dates and destination to compare available options.

Cheap Europe travel insurance: when a low price is genuinely good value

Cheap travel insurance can be perfectly suitable when the cover matches your needs. The problem is comparing only the premium. A lower price can come with a higher excess, lower cancellation limits, narrower medical wording or exclusions that matter to your trip.

Example: £18 policy vs £32 policy

If both cover your itinerary and the £18 policy has suitable medical, repatriation and cancellation terms, paying more is not automatically better. But if the cheaper option excludes an activity you plan to do or leaves most of your prepaid trip cost uninsured, the saving can be misleading.

Compare value, not only price

Use this order: eligibility → medical cover → repatriation → cancellation → excess → activities and exclusions → price.

Single-trip or annual travel insurance for Europe?

Both can work for UK travellers. A single-trip Europe policy is built around one journey and can be easier to compare when you know the exact destination, dates and trip cost. Annual travel insurance for Europe can suit people taking several trips during a year, but every journey still has to fit the policy rules.

Single-trip cover

Useful when you are protecting one specific holiday and want the policy dates, destination and cancellation value to match that journey.

Annual multi-trip cover

Useful for frequent travel, but check the maximum duration of each trip, geographic area, age limits and whether winter sports or other activities are included.

Do not assume “annual Europe” means every European destination or every trip length is automatically covered.

What counts as Europe for travel insurance?

This sounds simple, but insurers can define geographic areas differently. A policy labelled “Europe” may use its own country list or zones, and some destinations can be treated differently depending on the provider.

Before buying, check the actual policy territory rather than relying on geography alone. This is particularly relevant if your itinerary includes several countries or destinations such as Spain, Greece, Poland, Malta, Tenerife or Turkey. The question is not only whether a place is geographically in Europe; it is whether your insurer includes that destination in the selected policy region.

Pre-existing medical conditions: do not leave this until after you buy

Existing medical conditions can affect eligibility, price, exclusions and claims. A condition that is controlled by medication can still be relevant if the insurer asks about it. Conditions under investigation, recent hospital treatment, medication changes or pending tests can also matter depending on the policy wording.

If the insurer asks about your health, answer accurately. Do not decide for the insurer that a condition is too minor to mention. Our dedicated pre-existing medical conditions guide explains medical screening, pending tests, emergency treatment and cancellation in more detail.

UK traveller checklist before buying Europe travel insurance

  1. Check whether you have a valid GHIC or eligible EHIC for the countries you will visit.
  2. Confirm the insurer sells the policy to UK residents and covers every destination on your itinerary.
  3. Compare emergency medical treatment and medical repatriation separately.
  4. Calculate the prepaid, non-refundable cost of your trip before choosing cancellation cover.
  5. Check the policy excess and important per-item limits.
  6. Declare medical conditions accurately when asked.
  7. Confirm activities such as winter sports, cruises or cycling are covered.
  8. For annual policies, check maximum trip duration and geographic area.
  9. Save the insurer's assistance number and policy details offline.
  10. Read the current policy wording before purchase; the provider's live terms always prevail.

Frequently asked questions

Do UK travellers need travel insurance for Europe?

Travel insurance is not a universal entry requirement for ordinary UK tourism in Europe, but it can protect against costs that GHIC or EHIC do not cover, such as medical repatriation, cancellation, baggage loss and some private treatment. Check the destination rules and the policy wording for your trip.

Is a UK GHIC or EHIC the same as travel insurance?

No. A valid GHIC or eligible EHIC can help with medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries under local rules, but it is not a travel insurance policy and does not replace cover for risks such as repatriation, cancellation or baggage.

What should travel health insurance for Europe cover?

Key items to compare include emergency medical treatment, medical repatriation, 24/7 assistance, deductibles or excess, private-care rules, pre-existing-condition wording and any activity exclusions that apply to your trip.

Is annual or single-trip travel insurance better for Europe?

It depends on how often you travel. Single-trip insurance is designed for one journey, while annual multi-trip cover can suit frequent travellers. Compare maximum trip length, age limits, geographic area and activity restrictions before choosing.

Is cheap Europe travel insurance good enough?

A lower premium can still be good value, but compare what you receive for the price. Medical limits, repatriation, cancellation, excess, baggage, activities and exclusions can matter more than a small saving on the premium.

Do I need to declare pre-existing medical conditions?

If the insurer asks about your medical history, answer accurately. Definitions and screening rules vary, and a stable or controlled condition can still be relevant to eligibility, exclusions or claims.

Does Europe travel insurance automatically cover every European destination?

Not necessarily. Insurers can define Europe differently and may apply separate geographic zones or exclusions. Check the exact list of covered countries for your policy, especially for multi-country itineraries or destinations near the edge of a policy's Europe region.
