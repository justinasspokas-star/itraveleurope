Start by finding the gaps you already have

Canadian travelers can arrive at the same Europe itinerary with very different existing benefits. Before adding another policy, review your provincial or territorial plan, workplace coverage and any credit-card insurance linked to the trip.

The Government of Canada recommends checking policy details and the travel advice for your destination before booking and buying insurance. Existing public health coverage should not be assumed to pay the full cost of treatment abroad.

What to compare after checking existing benefits

Emergency medicalCheck the total limit, deductible and how claims or direct hospital payments work.
Medical evacuationRead where the insurer may transport you and whether a return to Canada is guaranteed or conditional.
Trip durationSome annual or credit-card policies cap the number of consecutive days outside your province.
Cancellation & interruptionMatch protection to prepaid non-refundable bookings and read covered reasons carefully.

Credit-card insurance: useful, but verify the activation rules

A card can provide valuable travel protection, but benefits often depend on how much of the trip was paid with the card, the traveler's age, the number of days away and whether a spouse or dependent is covered. Do not buy duplicate cover until you have read the certificate of insurance for the card you actually used.

  • Check age and trip-length limits.
  • Check whether medical coverage is included or only trip cancellation/delay.
  • Check whether the full fare or only part of the trip must be charged to the card.
  • Check exclusions for pre-existing medical conditions.

Why a Europe trip can expose different risks

Multi-country itineraries often mix separate airlines, trains, ferries and hotels. If one delay causes a missed independently booked connection, the wording for delay and interruption matters. Island trips, cruises and mountain routes also make evacuation language more important.

Frequently asked questions

Should Canadians buy travel medical insurance for Europe?

First confirm what your provincial or territorial plan, workplace plan and credit card already cover. Then compare additional protection for the gaps, especially emergency medical care and evacuation.

Can credit-card travel insurance be enough?

Sometimes, but it depends on the specific certificate. Trip-length limits, age restrictions, activation conditions and benefit amounts vary, so read the policy before relying on it.

Do Canadians need Schengen visa insurance?

For an ordinary short visa-free visit by a Canadian citizen, the formal Schengen visa-insurance requirement normally does not apply. A traveler who personally needs a visa because of nationality or immigration status should follow the consulate's rules.
