Schengen visa insurance requirements

Travel medical insurance for a Schengen visa is a document requirement, not simply an optional trip benefit. The policy must meet the Visa Code rules for minimum medical cover, repatriation, territory and the period of the intended stay. Your consulate or visa application centre can also require specific evidence or certificate formatting.

Core rule

What the EU Visa Code says the insurance must cover

Minimum coverAt least €30,000.
Medical eventsRepatriation for medical reasons, urgent medical attention, emergency hospital treatment or death.
TerritoryValid throughout the relevant Member States.
PeriodCover the entire intended stay or transit required by the visa rules.
Where to buyApplicants should in principle take out the insurance in their country of residence; if that is not possible, the Visa Code allows obtaining it elsewhere.
EU Visa Code, Article 15 ↗European Commission: applying for a Schengen visa ↗

Who this requirement applies to

Visa insurance is for the visa application—not every visitor to Europe.

Travelers who need a short-stay Schengen visa generally have to present travel medical insurance as part of the application. Visa-exempt tourists may still choose travel insurance, but that is a different decision from satisfying Article 15.

  1. Check whether you need a Schengen visa for your nationality and travel documents.
  2. Apply to the correct consulate based on your main destination and itinerary.
  3. Use the exact required dates for the intended stay when arranging evidence of insurance.
  4. Follow local instructions if the consulate or visa centre specifies certificate wording or an approved process.

Complete checklist

How to verify Schengen visa insurance before you submit it

Article 15 of the EU Visa Code is the starting point, but applicants also need a certificate that makes compliance easy to verify. The safest approach is to compare the legal requirements with the instructions of the consulate or visa centre handling your application.

The core Article 15 requirements

  • Minimum coverage: at least €30,000.
  • Covered medical events: repatriation for medical reasons or death, urgent medical attention and emergency hospital treatment.
  • Territory: valid throughout the relevant Member States.
  • Period: the intended stay or transit covered by the visa application.
  • Purchase location: applicants should in principle take out insurance in their country of residence when possible.

What should appear on the insurance certificate?

Consulates and visa centres need to be able to identify the traveler and confirm the cover. A useful certificate normally makes the following information easy to find:

TravelerFull name matching the passport or application documents.
PolicyInsurer and policy or certificate number.
DatesThe required insured period for the intended stay or transit.
TerritoryWording that clearly covers the required Schengen / Member State territory.
Medical limitAt least €30,000 in qualifying travel medical coverage.
RepatriationEvidence that medical repatriation / repatriation in case of death is included as required.

Common certificate mistakes

Insurance starts one day late

Your flight or entry date is 10 May, but the certificate begins on 11 May. The document no longer clearly covers the full intended period.

The territory is vague

A certificate says “international” or “Europe” without making the required Member State validity clear. Follow the consulate's document instructions rather than assuming marketing language is enough.

Only the policy summary is uploaded

A sales confirmation may not show the traveler's name, dates, territory and qualifying medical/repatriation benefits in a form the visa officer can verify.

Multiple-entry visas

Under Article 15, applicants for a multiple-entry visa must prove adequate and valid travel medical insurance for the period of the first intended visit and acknowledge the need to have insurance for subsequent stays. Do not assume one certificate automatically covers every future trip simply because the visa itself is valid for a longer period.

Family and group applications

When several travelers are insured under one policy, make sure every applicant who relies on the certificate can be identified clearly. If the insurer issues a schedule of insured persons, include the pages that show the names and relevant benefits rather than only the payment receipt.

When should you buy Schengen visa insurance?

Buy it early enough to have the required certificate for the application, but only after you understand the dates and territorial requirements. Check cancellation or refund rules in case the visa is refused or the trip changes. Those refund terms are contractual and separate from the Visa Code's minimum medical requirements.

Do visa-exempt travelers need Schengen visa insurance?

The Article 15 requirement applies to Schengen visa applicants. A visa-exempt tourist may still choose travel insurance for medical bills, evacuation, cancellation or baggage, but that is not the same legal requirement. Use our travel insurance for Europe guide for ordinary trip protection.

Before you purchase

Five details that can make a certificate easier to verify.

The insurance has to be demonstrably compliant—not merely marketed as “Europe cover”.

  1. Traveler name: match the passport exactly.
  2. Benefit limit: show at least €30,000 clearly.
  3. Territory: state the required Schengen/Member State validity.
  4. Dates: match the required period in the application.
  5. Repatriation: make sure it is expressly included.
