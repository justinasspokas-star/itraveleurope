Trip cancellation insurance for illness: what happens if you cannot travel to Europe?

If sickness, injury or a family medical emergency stops your trip before departure, travel insurance may reimburse some prepaid, non-refundable costs — but only when the reason is covered and the policy conditions are met.

By iTravelEurope Editorial TeamReviewed October 12, 2026Approx. 14 min read

Quick answer

Travel insurance can cover trip cancellation due to illness when the sickness or injury meets the policy definition of a covered reason. The insurer normally looks at four things: why you cancelled, when the event happened, whether the policy was already in force and how much of the trip cost you could not recover from airlines, hotels or other suppliers. A doctor's note alone does not automatically make every cancellation claim payable.

What does trip cancellation insurance cover?

Trip cancellation insurance is designed to protect prepaid, non-refundable travel costs when a covered event prevents you from starting the trip. Depending on the policy, covered reasons can include unexpected serious illness or injury of the traveler, a traveling companion or certain family members.

This is different from emergency medical insurance. Medical cover helps if you become sick or injured during the trip. Cancellation cover applies when a covered event stops the trip from starting.

Before departureTrip cancellation: you cannot start the trip because of a covered reason.
During the tripTrip interruption / curtailment: you have to end the trip early for a covered reason.
During the tripEmergency medical: you need treatment while travelling.

One policy may include all three benefits, but they usually have separate definitions, limits and exclusions.

Does travel insurance cover cancellation due to illness?

Often, yes — but not every illness is automatically a covered reason. A policy may require the condition to be serious, unexpected, medically documented and severe enough to prevent travel.

More likely to fit a covered reason

An unexpected illness requiring hospitalisation, a serious injury, or a doctor confirming that you are medically unfit to travel — if the wording includes that situation.

May not be enough on its own

A mild cold, routine appointment, fear of becoming ill, or simply deciding you no longer want to travel.

Needs extra checking

An illness connected to a pre-existing condition, symptoms that existed before the policy was bought, or a relative's medical condition.

The phrase “covered reason” matters. Standard trip cancellation insurance is not the same as being able to cancel for any reason.

What if a family member becomes ill before your Europe trip?

Many policies can include serious illness, injury, hospitalisation or death of a close family member, even if that person is not travelling. But the insurer decides who counts as a family member and how serious the medical event must be.

Check definitions for spouse or partner, children, parents, grandparents, siblings and in-laws. Do not assume every relative is included.

Example

You have a non-refundable flight and hotel package for Italy. Two days before departure, your father is unexpectedly hospitalised. Whether the cancellation is covered depends on the policy's family definition, the medical circumstances and any exclusions — not just on the fact that a hospital admission happened.

Pre-existing medical conditions can change the answer

If the illness that causes cancellation existed before you bought the policy, the claim can become more complicated. Some policies exclude pre-existing medical conditions. Others may cover them after medical screening, subject to stability requirements, additional premium or a specific waiver.

For this reason, the phrase trip cancellation insurance for pre-existing conditions should never be interpreted as automatically covered. Read the medical declaration and cancellation sections together.

When should you buy travel insurance with cancellation cover?

In general, buy travel insurance soon after you start committing non-refundable money to the trip. Waiting until a medical problem appears is too late to insure that known event.

Purchase timing can also affect access to benefits such as pre-existing-condition waivers or broader cancellation options in some markets. Policies differ, so check the deadline from the insurer rather than relying on a general rule.

  1. Book the flight, hotel, cruise or tour.
  2. Identify what part of the booking is non-refundable.
  3. Compare cancellation limits against that amount.
  4. Check any purchase deadline for special benefits.
  5. Declare medical information accurately when asked.

How much trip cancellation cover do you need?

Do not insure the headline price of the holiday without checking what is actually at risk. Focus on the prepaid, non-refundable trip cost.

Non-refundable flightUsually part of the cancellation exposure if the policy covers the reason.
Refundable hotelIf the hotel refunds you in full, there may be no insured loss for that booking.
Rail pass or tourCount the amount you would lose under the supplier's cancellation terms.
Pay-at-property bookingNormally not the same financial risk if no penalty applies.
Travel credits / vouchersCheck whether the insurer treats them as a recoverable value before calculating the claim.

If your trip has €5,000 of bookings but only €2,000 is genuinely non-refundable, those are different cancellation exposures.

Europe trip example: how reimbursement may work

Flights

You paid $1,200. The airline returns $300 in taxes and credits. Potential unrecovered loss: $900, subject to policy terms.

Hotels

You prepaid $1,000. One hotel refunds $600, another keeps a $250 penalty. Potential unrecovered loss: $250.

Tours

You paid $400 for two tours. One is fully refundable; the other keeps $150. Potential unrecovered loss: $150.

The insurer generally does not pay you twice for money you already recovered from the supplier. The claim focuses on covered, documented loss up to the policy limit.

What documents are usually needed for a cancellation claim?

Claim documentation is one of the most practical parts of trip cancellation insurance. Keep evidence from the moment you book.

  1. Policy certificate and policy wording.
  2. Booking confirmations showing traveler names, dates and cancellation terms.
  3. Proof of payment for flights, hotels, tours and other insured costs.
  4. Supplier cancellation statements showing what was refunded and what was kept.
  5. Medical evidence or other documentation proving the covered reason.
  6. Proof of relationship if the claim depends on illness of a family member and the insurer requests it.
  7. Claim form and insurer correspondence.

Do not cancel and discard the original confirmations. They may be needed to prove the amount you actually lost.

Should you cancel the bookings before making the insurance claim?

Usually you should take reasonable steps to reduce the loss. That means contacting the airline, hotel, cruise company or tour provider quickly and requesting any refund, credit or waiver available under their terms.

Then document what remains non-refundable. If you delay cancelling a booking and the penalty grows, the insurer may question whether the extra loss could have been avoided.

Airline refund rights are not the same as travel insurance

If the airline cancels your flight, your consumer rights may create a refund or rerouting entitlement. If you cancel because you are ill, your rights depend much more on the fare conditions and any applicable consumer protections.

That distinction matters for Europe trips. Travel insurance is generally designed to address the insured traveler's covered reason for cancellation, while airline passenger-rights rules deal with disruption caused by the carrier or qualifying operational events.

Always check the supplier refund first. Insurance should not be treated as a replacement for money the airline or hotel already owes you.

What is Cancel For Any Reason (CFAR)?

In some markets, especially the United States, an optional Cancel For Any Reason benefit can provide broader flexibility than standard named-reason cancellation cover.

CFAR normally has strict purchase deadlines, may require you to insure the full eligible trip cost and usually reimburses only a percentage of the loss rather than 100%. It is not available with every policy or in every jurisdiction.

UK travelers: cancellation cover and GHIC are different

A UK GHIC or valid EHIC can help with medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries, but it is not trip cancellation insurance. It will not reimburse your non-refundable flight or hotel because illness stops you travelling.

UK travelers should check cancellation or curtailment limits separately from medical cover, GHIC/EHIC entitlements and repatriation.

Common reasons a trip cancellation claim may be declined

  • The policy does not include cancellation cover.
  • The illness does not meet the policy definition of a covered reason.
  • Symptoms or a diagnosis were already known and excluded before the policy started.
  • The traveler did not disclose medical information when required.
  • The cancellation reason is fear, change of mind or another non-covered event.
  • The claim exceeds the cancellation benefit limit.
  • The traveler cannot prove the non-refundable loss.
  • The airline, hotel or tour provider already refunded the cost.
  • The traveler waited too long to cancel and allowed avoidable penalties to increase.

Trip cancellation checklist before you buy

  1. Add up prepaid, non-refundable trip costs.
  2. Make sure the cancellation limit is high enough.
  3. Read the exact definition of illness, injury and medically unfit to travel.
  4. Check family-member definitions if relatives' health is a concern.
  5. Review pre-existing medical condition rules.
  6. Check purchase deadlines and any waiting period.
  7. Understand the difference between cancellation and interruption.
  8. Check whether you need CFAR or standard named-reason cover.
  9. Keep every booking confirmation and cancellation term.
  10. Buy before a foreseeable problem becomes a known event.

Frequently asked questions

Does travel insurance cover trip cancellation due to illness?

It can when the illness meets the policy definition of a covered reason and the other conditions are satisfied. Not every illness automatically qualifies.

Can I cancel a Europe trip if a family member becomes ill?

Many policies include serious illness, injury or hospitalisation of certain family members, but definitions of family and severity vary. Check the exact wording.

Will travel insurance cover cancellation because of a pre-existing condition?

Sometimes, but not automatically. Some policies exclude pre-existing conditions, while others may cover them after medical screening or under a waiver or stability requirement.

Do I get all my money back if I cancel for illness?

Not necessarily. The insurer normally considers only eligible non-refundable losses, up to the policy limit, after supplier refunds or credits are taken into account.

Is a doctor's note enough for a cancellation claim?

A medical document can be important evidence, but the illness must still fit the policy's covered-reason definition and the insurer may require additional records or claim forms.

What is the difference between trip cancellation and trip interruption?

Cancellation applies before the trip starts. Interruption or curtailment applies after departure when a covered event forces you to shorten the trip.

What is Cancel For Any Reason coverage?

CFAR is a broader optional benefit available with some policies. It usually has strict purchase deadlines and normally reimburses only part of the insured trip cost.
