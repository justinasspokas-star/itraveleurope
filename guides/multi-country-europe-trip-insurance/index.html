One policy for a multi-country Europe trip

A multi-country trip is not difficult to insure because it crosses borders; it is difficult because one disruption can affect several independently booked reservations. Build the comparison around the chain of travel, not a list of capital cities.

iTravelEurope EditorialReviewed September 24, 2026

Declare the full route

Some quote flows ask for a primary destination, while others require every country. Follow the provider’s instructions and include the full travel period, including positioning nights when relevant. The UK and Ireland are not part of the Schengen Area, although a policy may still group them within a wider Europe region.

Territory names are insurer definitions, not geography lessons. Read which countries are included rather than assuming ‘Europe’ has one fixed meaning.

Map the reservation chain

List each flight, train, ferry, cruise, hotel and tour that depends on the previous segment. Then identify which items are refundable. This produces the trip cost and reveals risky connections.

A missed train caused by leaving too little transfer time is different from a documented airline delay that meets a policy’s covered-reason and waiting-period rules. Insurance does not make every self-built connection protected.

Compare delay and interruption precisely

Look for the waiting period, per-day limit, total limit, covered transport types and required documents. For interruption, check how unused arrangements and additional transport are calculated.

Keep cancellation notices, delay statements, receipts and rebooking confirmations. The insurer needs evidence of both the event and the financial loss.

Use one emergency plan

Save the insurer’s assistance number offline, share the policy with a travel companion and know when prior authorization is required. A single organized record is more useful than several overlapping products nobody can find during a disruption.
