Europe travel insurance for travelers over 65

Age can change eligibility, price and medical benefits, but it should not reduce the comparison to ‘the cheapest senior plan.’ The better method is to map health, trip cost and itinerary complexity to the policy wording.

iTravelEurope EditorialReviewed September 24, 2026

Separate medical risk from trip-cost risk

Emergency medical coverage pays eligible treatment expenses during the trip. Trip cancellation protects eligible prepaid costs when a covered event prevents departure. They solve different problems and often use different limits.

A traveler visiting family with flexible lodging may need less cancellation coverage than someone with a prepaid cruise. Conversely, a low-cost trip can still justify strong medical and evacuation benefits.

Check age limits at benefit level

A plan may accept your age while reducing a specific benefit or offering a different maximum. Review the schedule of benefits for emergency medical, accidental death, evacuation and trip length. Do not rely only on the comparison-card headline.

Also check whether the premium changes when a birthday occurs before departure and whether all travelers must be insured on the same policy.

Handle pre-existing conditions early

If a plan offers a pre-existing-condition exclusion waiver, it usually comes with timing and eligibility conditions. That can make the date of the first trip payment important. Waiting until the week before departure may remove an option that existed earlier.

A waiver does not turn travel insurance into routine international health insurance. Expected care, ongoing treatment and travel against medical advice may remain outside coverage.

Design the itinerary to reduce avoidable claims

Leave recovery time after an overnight flight, avoid tight self-booked connections and carry medication in original labeled packaging. Keep a current medication list and physician contact. Insurance works better when the itinerary itself has sensible margins.
