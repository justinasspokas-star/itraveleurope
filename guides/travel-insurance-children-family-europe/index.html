Travel insurance for children & babies in Europe

When you travel with a child, a high medical limit is only part of the story. Good family travel insurance should be checked for pediatric emergencies, hospital stays, a parent staying with the child, what happens if the parent is hospitalized, medical repatriation and pre-existing conditions.

Written and reviewed by iTravelEurope Editorial TeamReviewed October 24, 2026Approx. 15 min read

Quick answer

Children and babies should be explicitly included in the travel-insurance arrangement if you want them covered. Do not assume a baby is automatically insured because a parent has a policy. Before buying, confirm every traveler, the child's medical limit, emergency pediatric treatment, hospital accompaniment, repatriation and any rules for chronic or congenital conditions.

Do children need travel insurance for Europe?

Children can become ill, need urgent treatment, lose baggage or force a family to cancel or interrupt a trip just as adults can. The insurance question is therefore less about whether a child “needs a separate adult-style policy” and more about whether the child is correctly included under the chosen family or group policy.

When comparing family travel insurance for Europe, check the traveler list before payment. A child who is missing from the certificate or covered-person definition can create a serious problem at claim time.

Can you buy travel insurance for children only?

Sometimes, but eligibility varies by insurer. A child may be covered under a family policy, added to an adult's policy, or in some cases insured under a child-only or group arrangement. Do not assume a child can be insured alone simply because the quote form accepts their age.

If a child is travelling without both parents — for example with grandparents, another relative, a school group or one parent — check who must be named on the policy, whether an accompanying adult is required, who can authorize treatment and how emergency assistance or repatriation would work.

For “children only” cover, verify four things:

Who is the insured person, who is the supervising adult, who can make medical decisions, and who the insurer will contact if the child cannot continue the trip.

Pediatric emergency abroad: what matters more than the headline medical limit

A policy may advertise a large medical limit, but families should also check how assistance actually works. Who do you call? Can the insurer direct you to an appropriate clinic? Is there cashless treatment or might you need to pay first? Does the policy require authorization for a private hospital?

For a child, the practical details can matter as much as the limit because parents often need fast guidance about where to go and whether the facility can treat pediatric patients.

Useful question to ask before buying

“If my child develops a high fever or needs urgent pediatric assessment in Europe, how do I contact your assistance team, where would you send us, and would I have to pay upfront?”

What if your child is hospitalized in Europe?

Do not stop at “hospital treatment covered.” A family can face additional costs if one parent needs to stay near the hospital, extend accommodation, change flights or bring another relative to help with siblings.

Child hospitalized

Check the child's medical limit, parent accommodation, additional transport and repatriation arrangements.

Hospital stay extends beyond the trip

Check whether the policy can extend cover, rearrange return travel or pay eligible additional accommodation.

Siblings are travelling too

Ask how the insurer handles the rest of the family if one parent must remain with the hospitalized child.

What if a parent is hospitalized and the children are well?

This is one of the most overlooked family travel-insurance questions. If a parent is admitted to hospital and cannot care for dependent children, someone may need to supervise them or accompany them home.

Some family policies can include travel for another adult, supervision or repatriation of dependent children. The benefit name and conditions vary, so search the policy wording rather than assuming “repatriation” automatically includes the children.

EHIC for children: each family member needs their own card

Families eligible for the European Health Insurance Card should not assume one parent's EHIC covers everyone. European Commission guidance says each family member should have their own card.

EHIC can help eligible children access medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay under the rules of the country being visited. It does not replace family travel insurance and does not cover private healthcare or medical repatriation.

Travel insurance for babies: five extra checks

  1. Age eligibility. Confirm that the insurer can cover the baby's age and that the date of birth is entered correctly.
  2. Emergency pediatric care. Know how to reach assistance and where the insurer would direct you.
  3. Hospital accompaniment. Check whether a parent can remain with the baby if hospital admission is required.
  4. Pushchair and baby equipment. Check baggage definitions and limits for strollers or essential child equipment.
  5. Existing medical issues. If the baby was premature, has a congenital condition or needs ongoing treatment, check the pre-existing-condition wording before buying.

Pre-existing and congenital conditions in children

A child's asthma, diabetes, epilepsy, heart condition, congenital diagnosis or condition under investigation may be relevant to travel insurance even if it is well controlled at home.

Policies can use different definitions, screening rules and exclusions. Do not decide that a condition is “too minor” to mention if the insurer asks about it.

Medication for children: prepare before the trip

The CDC recommends that caregivers travelling internationally with children who need medication for chronic conditions carry an adequate supply. Keep medicines in their original packaging where practical, carry enough for delays, and have a list of the generic medicine names rather than relying only on a brand name.

Insurance is not a substitute for routine medication planning. A policy may reimburse medicine prescribed because of a covered emergency, while replacement of forgotten or exhausted everyday medication can be treated differently.

Vaccines and pre-travel health planning for children

The CDC Yellow Book recommends reviewing routine childhood vaccinations before international travel and assessing whether destination-specific vaccines or preventive medicines are needed. The NHS similarly advises travelers, including children, to be up to date with routine vaccines and to seek travel-health advice early enough before departure when additional vaccines may be required.

The right advice depends on destination, age, medical history and planned activities. Insurance does not replace a pre-travel health assessment.

Family trip cancellation: what if a child gets sick before departure?

If a child becomes seriously ill before the trip, cancellation cover may be relevant. But emergency medical insurance during the trip and cancellation cover before departure are separate benefits.

Family travel insurance: one family policy or separate policies?

There is no universal winner. A family policy can be convenient and sometimes cheaper, while separate policies can be useful when travelers have very different medical histories, trip lengths or insurance needs.

Family policyCheck who qualifies as family, whether children must live at the same address, age limits for dependents and whether adults must travel together.
Separate policiesMay make sense when one traveler needs specialist medical screening, higher cancellation cover or a different trip duration.
Annual family coverCheck maximum trip length, whether children can travel without both parents and which geographic region applies.

Multi-country Europe trips with children

A family itinerary can combine flights, trains, ferries and separately booked hotels. That makes missed connections and trip interruption more important because one disruption can affect several travelers at once.

Check whether all countries are inside the policy territory and whether self-transfer connections are protected.

What to do if your child needs medical care during the trip

  1. For a serious emergency in the EU, call 112.
  2. Contact the insurer's assistance service as soon as reasonably possible.
  3. Ask where to go if the situation is urgent but not life-threatening.
  4. Show the child's EHIC/GHIC when eligible and using the public system.
  5. Keep medical reports, prescriptions and itemised receipts.
  6. Tell the insurer immediately about hospital admission so accompaniment, extended stay or repatriation can be discussed.
  7. Do not arrange expensive private transport or evacuation without authorization unless waiting would be unsafe.

Family travel insurance checklist for Europe

  1. Confirm every adult, child and baby is included.
  2. Check the medical-expense limit per person.
  3. Check pediatric assistance and telemedicine availability.
  4. Check parent accommodation if a child is hospitalized.
  5. Check child supervision if a parent is hospitalized.
  6. Check accompanied repatriation of dependent children.
  7. Review pre-existing and congenital-condition wording.
  8. Check family cancellation limits against your total non-refundable cost.
  9. Give each eligible family member their own EHIC/GHIC.
  10. Carry medicines, prescriptions and important medical information.
  11. Check stroller and child-equipment baggage limits.
  12. Save the insurer's emergency number offline before departure.

Frequently asked questions

Do children need travel insurance for Europe?

Children should be explicitly covered if you want the policy to respond to their medical and travel risks. Check that every child appears on the policy or falls clearly within its insured-family definition.

Can I buy travel insurance for a child without a parent?

Sometimes, but eligibility varies by insurer. Check whether child-only cover is allowed, who must be named on the policy, which adult is responsible during the trip and how emergency treatment or repatriation would be authorized.

Does family travel insurance cover pediatric care?

Emergency medical care for an insured child may be covered, but dedicated pediatric services, telemedicine and family support benefits vary by insurer.

What happens if my child is hospitalized abroad?

Check the child's medical limit plus parent accommodation, additional travel, extended stay and repatriation benefits. Do not assume all of these are included just because hospital treatment is covered.

What happens to my children if I am hospitalized?

Some family policies can arrange or reimburse supervision, travel of another adult or accompanied repatriation of dependent children. The exact wording matters.

Does my child need their own EHIC?

Yes, if eligible. European Commission guidance says each family member should have their own card.

Are congenital or pre-existing conditions covered?

Not automatically. They can be excluded, limited or subject to medical screening and disclosure requirements.

Does travel insurance cover a stroller?

It depends on the baggage definition, limits, depreciation rules and cause of loss or damage. Check whether strollers or child equipment are specifically addressed.
