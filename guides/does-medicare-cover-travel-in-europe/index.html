Does Medicare cover travel in Europe?

Usually not in the way many U.S. travelers expect. Original Medicare generally provides very limited healthcare coverage outside the United States. Medicare Advantage, Medigap and retiree benefits can change the answer, so the useful question is not simply “Do I have Medicare?” but “Which coverage will actually respond in Europe?”

iTravelEurope Editorial TeamReviewed October 3, 2026

Quick answer

For a trip to Spain, France, Italy, Portugal, Greece or another European destination, do not assume your Medicare card works like it does at home. Medicare says Original Medicare usually does not cover healthcare outside the U.S. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer an extra overseas emergency benefit, and some Medigap plans include foreign travel emergency coverage up to plan limits.

Start with the type of Medicare coverage you actually have

“Medicare” can describe several very different combinations of benefits. Two travelers of the same age can have completely different protection abroad because one has Original Medicare plus Medigap while another has Medicare Advantage. Before comparing travel insurance, identify the exact cards and plans you carry.

Original Medicare: why Europe is usually outside the coverage zone

Medicare considers the 50 states, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, Northern Mariana Islands and American Samoa part of the United States. Europe is outside that area. Medicare lists only narrow situations in which foreign hospital, doctor or ambulance services may be covered, such as when a foreign hospital is closer than the nearest suitable U.S. hospital or certain travel through Canada between Alaska and another U.S. state.

Those exceptions are important legally, but they are not a practical safety net for a normal vacation in Madrid, Paris or Rome. For most ordinary European travel, the planning assumption should be that Original Medicare will not pay the medical bill.

Cruise exception does not mean “European cruise coverage.” Medicare describes limited cruise-ship coverage when the ship is in a U.S. port or no more than six hours from a U.S. port and other conditions are met. A Mediterranean cruise should not be assumed covered just because it is a cruise.

Medicare Advantage: the answer is plan-specific

Medicare states that Medicare Advantage plans generally do not cover medical care outside the U.S., although some plans may offer an extra benefit for emergency or urgently needed services while traveling abroad. That makes your plan's Evidence of Coverage more useful than a generic internet answer.

Before departure, call the number on your plan card and ask precise questions:

  • Is emergency care outside the United States covered?
  • Is urgently needed care covered, or only a medical emergency?
  • Do I pay the hospital first and claim reimbursement later?
  • Is there a deductible, coinsurance or maximum benefit?
  • Is medical evacuation or repatriation included?
  • Is pre-authorization required when I am medically able to call?
  • What documents will you require for a foreign claim?

Write down the answer, date and representative name, and keep a copy of the relevant plan wording with your travel documents.

Medigap foreign travel emergency coverage

Medigap can materially change the picture. Medicare's current plan comparison shows a foreign travel emergency benefit at 80%—up to plan limits—for standardized Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M and N. Not every person can buy Plans C or F; Medicare notes that eligibility depends on when you first became eligible for Medicare.

Do not interpret “80%” as unlimited worldwide medical insurance. The phrase up to plan limits matters. Ask your Medigap carrier about deductibles, time limits, lifetime or policy maximums, claim documentation and whether you may need to pay the European provider first.

Medical evacuation is a different risk from the hospital bill

A policy can help with treatment and still leave a major gap: transportation. If a serious illness or injury requires transfer to another facility or return to the United States, the logistics can be expensive and medically complex. Original Medicare should not be treated as a broad European medical evacuation benefit.

When comparing travel insurance, look beyond the headline “medical coverage” number. Check emergency medical transportation, evacuation, repatriation, assistance-provider authorization rules and who decides whether a transfer is medically necessary. Those details can matter more than a small difference in premium.

Prescription drugs: plan before you leave

Medicare states that Medicare drug plans do not cover prescription drugs purchased outside the United States. Carry enough medication for the planned trip when legally permitted, keep medicines in appropriately labeled packaging and check destination rules for controlled or restricted medicines.

If a medication is lost or you need a replacement abroad, a travel assistance service may help locate a doctor or pharmacy, but that is not the same as Medicare Part D paying for the purchase.

What happens if you need a hospital in Europe?

In an emergency, get appropriate care first. From an insurance perspective, expect the claims process to look different from a normal U.S. visit. Medicare notes that foreign hospitals are not required to file Medicare claims. Even where you have another benefit that responds, a provider may request payment or a deposit before you later seek reimbursement.

Minor urgent problem

You visit a clinic for an infection or injury. Ask for an itemized invoice, diagnosis, proof of payment and medical notes if available.

Hospital admission

Contact the assistance number on any travel, retiree or Medicare Advantage coverage as soon as reasonably possible. Some policies coordinate care or payment only after notification.

Need for transfer

Do not arrange an expensive medical flight independently unless circumstances require immediate action. Evacuation benefits commonly involve the insurer or assistance provider in deciding and arranging transport.

Travel insurance for Medicare travelers: what to compare

The goal is not to duplicate benefits blindly. First map what you already have, then compare the remaining risks. For many older travelers, the most important questions are medical limits, evacuation, pre-existing-condition terms, trip cancellation and the claims process.

Emergency medical

Check the maximum, deductible and whether coverage is primary or secondary.

Medical evacuation

Read who authorizes transport, destination rules and benefit limits.

Pre-existing conditions

Look for eligibility rules, exclusions, stability periods or waivers in the actual policy.

Trip cancellation

Insure only the prepaid, non-refundable costs you genuinely stand to lose and confirm covered reasons.

Trip interruption

Check extra transport and unused trip costs if a covered event ends the trip early.

24/7 assistance

Save the international assistance number offline before leaving the U.S.

A practical pre-Europe checklist for Medicare travelers

  1. Identify whether you have Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage.
  2. If you have Medigap, confirm the exact plan letter and foreign travel benefit.
  3. Ask any retiree or employer plan what it covers outside the U.S.
  4. Confirm how emergency medical bills are paid and reimbursed.
  5. Check evacuation and repatriation separately from medical treatment.
  6. Review pre-existing-condition wording before buying travel insurance.
  7. Carry enough prescription medication and verify destination rules.
  8. Save assistance numbers, policy numbers and plan documents offline.
  9. Keep itemized medical bills and proof of payment if treatment occurs.
  10. Tell a travel companion where your insurance information is stored.

Frequently asked questions

Does Medicare cover emergency medical care in Europe?

Original Medicare generally does not cover healthcare outside the United States, except in limited situations. Some Medicare Advantage plans may offer emergency or urgently needed coverage abroad, and some Medigap plans include a foreign travel emergency benefit up to plan limits.

Does Medicare Advantage cover international travel?

Some Medicare Advantage plans may offer an extra benefit for emergency or urgently needed services outside the United States, but benefits, networks, claim procedures and limits vary by plan. Travelers should verify their own Evidence of Coverage before departure.

Which Medigap plans include foreign travel emergency coverage?

Medicare's current comparison chart shows an 80% foreign travel emergency benefit, up to plan limits, for standardized Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M and N. Eligibility for Plans C and F is restricted for some people who became newly eligible for Medicare on or after January 1, 2020.

Does Medicare pay for prescriptions bought in Europe?

Medicare states that Medicare drug plans do not cover prescription drugs purchased outside the United States. Travelers should plan medication supplies and check country rules before departure.

Does Medicare cover medical evacuation from Europe to the United States?

Original Medicare generally does not provide a broad medical evacuation benefit from Europe to the United States. Travelers who want evacuation or repatriation protection should check whether it is included in another health benefit or travel insurance policy.

Do I need travel insurance if I have Medicare?

That depends on the benefits you already have and the risks you want to protect. Because Medicare coverage abroad is limited, many travelers compare travel medical, evacuation, cancellation and interruption benefits before a European trip.
