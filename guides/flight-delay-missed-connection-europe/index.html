Flight delay & missed connection in Europe: EU261 vs travel insurance

A delayed flight in Europe can trigger airline rights, travel-insurance benefits, both — or neither. The key is knowing who caused the loss, whether the flights are on one reservation and which costs the airline is already responsible for before you make an insurance claim.

By iTravelEurope Editorial TeamReviewed October 12, 2026

Quick answer

If EU air passenger rights apply, claim from the airline first for the rights it owes you — such as care, rerouting, reimbursement or compensation. Travel insurance is not a replacement for those airline obligations. It may help with separate, eligible losses such as certain missed connections, additional transport, accommodation or other delay costs when the policy wording covers them.

EU261 vs travel insurance: the simplest way to think about it

Airline / EU passenger rightsFocus on the airline's legal duties when a covered flight is delayed, cancelled, overbooked or causes a protected missed connection.
Travel insuranceFocuses on the benefits named in your policy, such as travel delay, missed departure, missed connection, trip interruption or additional accommodation.
Both can matterYou may have an airline right and an insurance claim for different losses, but you should not expect to be paid twice for the same expense.

For SEO searches such as EU261 flight delay compensation, missed connection compensation Europe and travel insurance flight delay Europe, this distinction is the most important starting point.

When do EU air passenger rights apply?

Under the current EU framework, passenger rights can apply to travelers of any nationality when the flight falls within the relevant geographic and airline rules.

Flight within the EUCovered whether the operating airline is EU or non-EU.
Flight departing the EU for a non-EU countryCovered whether the operating airline is EU or non-EU.
Flight arriving in the EU from outside the EUCovered when operated by an EU airline.
Iceland, Norway and SwitzerlandThe EU passenger-rights framework also applies to flights to and from these countries under the current official guidance.

If your flight is from New York to Paris on a U.S. airline, the inbound flight is not treated the same as Paris to New York on that same airline. Direction and operating carrier matter.

Current EU261-style compensation for long arrival delays

As of October 2026, the current rules remain in force. Eligible passengers who reach the final destination with a qualifying long delay can be entitled to compensation when the delay is not caused by extraordinary circumstances.

€250Flights of 1,500 km or less when the qualifying arrival delay threshold is met.
€400Intra-EU flights over 1,500 km and other eligible flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km when the qualifying delay threshold is met.
€600Certain eligible flights over 3,500 km when the qualifying long-delay threshold is met.

The exact result depends on distance, route, arrival delay and whether the carrier can prove extraordinary circumstances. For some rerouted long-distance journeys the amount can be reduced.

Important

The three-hour rule is about arrival at the final destination, not simply how late the aircraft pushed back from the gate.

What happens if you miss a connecting flight?

Under the current official EU guidance, a missed connection can qualify for compensation when:

  1. The flights were booked as part of a single reservation.
  2. EU air passenger-rights rules apply to the journey.
  3. You reach the final destination more than three hours late.
  4. The delay was not caused by extraordinary circumstances.

That is why one ticket from Madrid → Frankfurt → New York is legally different from buying Madrid → Frankfurt and Frankfurt → New York as two unrelated bookings.

Self-transfer on separate tickets: the biggest missed-connection trap

A self-transfer can be cheap, but it transfers more disruption risk to you. If the first flight is late and the second flight is a separate reservation, the second airline may treat you as a no-show rather than as a protected connecting passenger.

Protected connection

Barcelona → Amsterdam → Toronto on one reservation. A delay to the first flight makes you miss the second. The journey is assessed as a connected itinerary under the applicable rules.

Self-transfer

Barcelona → Amsterdam on ticket A, Amsterdam → Toronto on ticket B. If ticket A is late, ticket B may not be protected by the first airline's connection obligations.

Insurance question

Check whether the policy specifically covers missed connection or missed departure on separately booked transport, what minimum connection time applies and which causes qualify.

Airline care during a long delay

Cash compensation is only one part of passenger rights. Depending on flight distance and delay, airline assistance can begin after roughly two, three or four hours. That assistance can include refreshments and food, and if an overnight stay becomes necessary, hotel accommodation and transport between the airport and hotel.

If the airline does not provide required care and you reasonably pay for it yourself, keep itemised receipts. Official EU guidance says necessary, reasonable and appropriate expenses may be reimbursable.

If departure is delayed by at least five hours and you decide not to travel, the current rules can also create a right to ticket reimbursement and, where relevant, a return flight to the original departure airport.

When travel insurance may help with a flight delay

Travel insurance benefits vary widely. Do not search only for the phrase “flight delay covered”; read the trigger and the expense definition.

Travel delayMay pay a fixed benefit or eligible meals, accommodation or transport after a minimum delay period.
Missed connectionMay cover reasonable additional transport or accommodation when a covered delay causes a qualifying missed onward journey.
Missed departureCan be a separate benefit with different covered reasons, such as certain public-transport failures or accidents en route to the airport.
Trip interruptionApplies when a covered event after departure forces you to change or end the trip, not simply because a flight is a few hours late.
Additional accommodationMay be covered when the policy trigger is met and the airline or supplier is not already responsible for that same cost.

Extraordinary circumstances: no cash compensation does not always mean no rights

Adverse weather, air-traffic-management decisions, political instability and security risks can qualify as extraordinary circumstances. In those cases, the airline may not owe the standard cash compensation if it can prove the legal test is met.

That does not automatically erase every airline obligation. Care, rerouting or reimbursement rights can still matter depending on the situation.

Official EU guidance also notes that many routine technical problems and internal airline staff strikes are not automatically treated as extraordinary circumstances.

What to do at the airport when your flight is delayed

  1. Screenshot the delay. Save the airline app, departure board and any text or email notice.
  2. Ask the airline for the reason. Get the explanation in writing if possible.
  3. Ask about rerouting before buying a new ticket yourself.
  4. Request meals or hotel assistance when your waiting time qualifies.
  5. Keep every receipt. Meals, hotel, taxis, trains and replacement transport can become important evidence.
  6. Keep both boarding passes if a connection is involved.
  7. Record your actual final arrival time.
  8. Do not accept a voucher without understanding what rights you may be giving up.
  9. Claim from the airline first for amounts the airline is legally responsible for.
  10. Then check your travel-insurance policy for additional eligible losses.

Which receipts and documents should you keep?

  • Booking confirmation and ticket number.
  • Boarding passes for every sector.
  • Proof that all connecting flights were on one reservation, where relevant.
  • Airline delay or cancellation notice.
  • Written rerouting or rebooking information.
  • Itemised meal and refreshment receipts.
  • Hotel invoice and transport receipts.
  • Replacement flight, rail or bus ticket if you had to buy one.
  • Proof of the actual time you arrived at your final destination.
  • Airline response to your compensation or reimbursement claim.

EU261 compensation vs insurance reimbursement: an example

Airline side

Your eligible flight reaches Rome more than three hours late because of an airline-controlled problem. You may have a passenger-rights compensation claim plus care rights, depending on distance and circumstances.

Insurance side

The delay also causes you to lose a prepaid tour the next morning. The airline compensation does not automatically reimburse that tour. Whether travel insurance pays depends on the policy's travel-delay or missed-connection wording.

No double recovery

If the airline already reimburses your hotel, do not assume the insurer will also pay the same hotel invoice. Insurance generally focuses on remaining eligible loss.

What if the airline cancels the flight instead?

Cancellation creates a different set of choices. Under the current EU rules, the airline generally has to offer a choice between ticket reimbursement, rerouting to the final destination at the earliest opportunity, or rerouting later under comparable conditions subject to availability.

Cash compensation can also apply in some cancellations, but notice periods, alternative transport and extraordinary circumstances affect eligibility.

Travel insurance may still matter for losses outside the airline's responsibility, such as prepaid arrangements that cannot be recovered, subject to the policy wording.

Important 2026 update: EU air passenger rules are changing

The EU adopted revised air-passenger rules in July 2026. The European Commission says the new framework will strengthen areas including rerouting, claims, connecting journeys, baggage information and support for passengers with reduced mobility.

However, as of October 12, 2026, the existing rules described in this guide still apply. The Commission says the revised rules should enter into force next summer. We will update this guide when the new framework becomes applicable.

Flight delay insurance checklist before a Europe trip

  1. Check whether travel delay is a fixed benefit or expense reimbursement.
  2. Check the minimum number of delayed hours before cover begins.
  3. Read missed-connection and missed-departure as separate benefits.
  4. Check whether self-transfers on separate tickets are eligible.
  5. Check minimum scheduled connection-time rules.
  6. Check whether strikes, weather and mechanical breakdown are covered causes.
  7. Check the per-person and per-trip limit.
  8. Check whether receipts are mandatory.
  9. Understand what the airline must pay before claiming on insurance.
  10. Save the insurer's 24-hour assistance number before departure.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get EU261 compensation for a three-hour flight delay?

Potentially. Under the current rules, compensation can apply when you reach the final destination at least three hours late, EU air passenger rights apply and the carrier cannot rely on extraordinary circumstances. Distance and route affect the amount.

Does EU261 cover a missed connection?

It can when the connecting flights are on a single reservation, EU passenger rights apply and you arrive at the final destination more than three hours late because of the disruption, subject to the extraordinary-circumstances rules.

What if my connection is on a separate ticket?

The standard EU missed-connection protection is tied to a single reservation. Separate tickets create a self-transfer risk, so check the next airline's fare rules and your travel-insurance missed-connection wording.

Can I claim from both the airline and travel insurance?

You may have rights against both for different losses, but you should not expect duplicate reimbursement of the same cost. Claim airline obligations first and then document any remaining insured loss.

What if bad weather caused the delay?

Bad weather can qualify as an extraordinary circumstance, which may remove the standard cash-compensation obligation. Airline care, rerouting or reimbursement rights can still be relevant.

Are the EU flight-delay rules changing in 2027?

Revised rules were adopted in July 2026. The European Commission says current rules continue to apply for now and the revised framework should enter into force next summer.
