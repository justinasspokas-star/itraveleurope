GHIC vs travel insurance: what each covers in Europe

A UK GHIC and travel insurance solve different problems. GHIC can help eligible UK residents access medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries. Travel insurance can address risks that sit outside that public-healthcare system, including repatriation, cancellation, baggage and travel disruption.

iTravelEurope Editorial TeamReviewed October 3, 2026

Quick answer

Take a valid GHIC if you are eligible, but do not treat it as travel insurance. NHS and GOV.UK guidance make clear that GHIC is not a replacement for a travel policy. It does not cover private healthcare or medical repatriation, and it does not protect your flights, hotel bookings or baggage.

What a UK GHIC is designed to cover

NHS guidance says a UK GHIC or eligible UK EHIC gives access to medically necessary state-provided healthcare in the country you are visiting. “Medically necessary” means treatment that cannot reasonably wait until you return to the UK, with the healthcare provider in the destination deciding what is necessary.

That can include:

  • Emergency treatment and A&E.
  • Treatment for a long-term or pre-existing medical condition.
  • Routine medical care for pre-existing conditions that need monitoring.
  • Routine maternity care when the purpose of travel is not to give birth.
  • Oxygen therapy and kidney dialysis, although some treatments need to be arranged in advance.

Not all state healthcare is free. You can be charged the same statutory patient charges that a resident would pay, such as certain prescription or treatment costs.

What GHIC does not cover

Private healthcareGHIC is linked to the state healthcare system. A private doctor or hospital is not covered simply because you are in a GHIC country.
Medical repatriationThe card does not pay to transport you back to the UK because of illness, injury or death.
CancellationIt does not reimburse prepaid flights, hotels, cruises or tours when you cannot travel.
BaggageIt does not cover lost, stolen or delayed luggage.
Travel disruptionFlight delays, missed departures and extra accommodation are not GHIC healthcare benefits.
Rescue costsGOV.UK warns that travel insurance may be needed for risks such as mountain rescue.

Why private healthcare can catch travelers out

In tourist areas, a hotel or tour operator may call a private doctor or private ambulance. A GHIC is not accepted for private care. GOV.UK's Spain health guidance specifically warns that hotels may direct guests to private providers and that GHIC/EHIC will not be accepted there.

If the situation is not life-threatening, ask whether the provider is part of the state system. If you have travel insurance, contact its medical assistance team as soon as reasonably possible; they may direct you to an appropriate facility and explain the payment process.

Pre-existing conditions: GHIC can be useful, but it is not the whole answer

One of the most misunderstood parts of GHIC is pre-existing healthcare. NHS guidance says medically necessary care can include treatment or routine monitoring for long-term or pre-existing conditions. That is valuable, but it does not turn the card into a comprehensive travel insurance policy.

If you have an ongoing condition, also check:

  • Whether treatment needs to be arranged in advance, such as dialysis or chemotherapy.
  • Whether your travel insurance excludes, includes or requires declaration of the condition.
  • Medication rules for the destination.
  • What happens if illness forces you to cancel or cut the trip short.
  • Whether repatriation or an accompanying person's extra travel costs are covered.

GHIC, EHIC and the Provisional Replacement Certificate

Some UK residents still hold a valid UK EHIC, while others use a UK GHIC. Eligibility and country coverage can differ, so check current NHS guidance for the card you hold and the destination you are visiting.

If you are entitled to healthcare cover but need treatment and do not have your card available, an eligible traveler may be able to obtain a Provisional Replacement Certificate (PRC). NHSBSA says the PRC provides the same cover that an existing GHIC or eligible EHIC would normally provide in applicable countries.

Do you need travel insurance if you have GHIC?

For most travelers, this is the wrong either/or question. A GHIC can be a useful public-healthcare entitlement while travel insurance addresses broader financial risks. GOV.UK explicitly says GHIC and EHIC are not alternatives to travel insurance.

When comparing a travel policy, look for the gaps GHIC leaves:

Emergency medical

Check how the policy interacts with GHIC and whether it expects you to use state care where available.

Repatriation

Confirm emergency medical transport and return-to-UK benefits.

Cancellation

Match the limit to your non-refundable trip cost and read covered reasons.

Interruption

Check extra transport and unused trip costs after a covered event.

Baggage

Review item limits, excesses, valuables and reporting requirements.

Activities

Check skiing, hiking, cycling, cruises or other planned activities.

Example: a week in Spain

You need urgent state healthcare

Present your valid GHIC or eligible EHIC at the state provider. You receive treatment on the same basis as someone insured under the local state system, which can still involve patient charges.

Your hotel sends a private doctor

GHIC does not cover private care. A travel insurer may help if the treatment is eligible under the policy.

You become too ill to fly home normally

GHIC does not cover medical repatriation. This is where a separate travel policy's evacuation or repatriation benefit can become important.

You cancel before departure

GHIC has no cancellation benefit. A travel policy may respond if the reason is covered and the costs are insured.

Three practical Europe scenarios

UK traveler breaks an ankle in Spain

GHIC can be relevant for medically necessary state healthcare where the card is accepted. If the traveler needs private treatment, additional accommodation or medically necessary transport home, those are separate questions for the travel policy.

Flight is cancelled before departure

GHIC does nothing here because there is no healthcare event. Whether unrecoverable hotel, flight or tour costs are covered depends on the travel policy's cancellation wording and the reason for cancellation.

Traveler needs transport back to the UK after treatment

Access to state healthcare and repatriation are different protections. The travel insurer's medical assistance team normally controls whether repatriation is medically necessary and how it is arranged.

What to carry while travelling

  1. Your valid GHIC/EHIC when eligible.
  2. Your travel-insurance certificate and policy number.
  3. The insurer's 24/7 assistance contact details.
  4. A list of medications and relevant medical information.
  5. Receipts and reports if you pay for treatment or incur extra insured costs.

UK traveler checklist before Europe

  1. Check that your GHIC or eligible EHIC is valid for the destination and travel dates.
  2. Check destination-specific state healthcare rules and possible patient charges.
  3. Arrange any regular treatment that needs advance coordination.
  4. Compare travel insurance for private medical care, repatriation and trip risks.
  5. Declare medical conditions accurately when the insurer asks.
  6. Save the insurer's assistance number and your policy details offline.
  7. Know the difference between state and private healthcare at your destination.
  8. Keep receipts and medical documentation if you pay for treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need travel insurance if I have a GHIC?

A GHIC is not a replacement for travel insurance. It can provide access to medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries, but it does not cover risks such as private healthcare, medical repatriation, trip cancellation or baggage loss.

What does a GHIC cover in Europe?

A valid UK GHIC can provide access to medically necessary state-provided healthcare in participating countries. This can include emergency treatment and certain treatment for pre-existing conditions, but local patient charges may still apply.

Does GHIC cover pre-existing medical conditions?

NHS guidance states that medically necessary healthcare can include treatment or routine medical care for long-term or pre-existing conditions. Some planned treatments, such as dialysis or chemotherapy, need to be arranged with the provider in advance.

Does GHIC cover private hospitals?

No. GHIC is for eligible state-provided healthcare. Private healthcare is not covered by the card.

Does GHIC cover medical repatriation to the UK?

No. NHS and GOV.UK guidance state that GHIC does not cover the cost of transporting someone back to the UK because of illness, injury or death.

What if I need treatment but do not have my GHIC with me?

If you are eligible, a Provisional Replacement Certificate (PRC) can provide temporary evidence of the same healthcare entitlement in applicable countries. NHSBSA explains how to request one.

What is the difference between GHIC and EHIC?

Both cards can provide access to medically necessary state healthcare under applicable reciprocal arrangements. Which card you hold and where it is valid depends on eligibility and destination; check current NHS guidance before travel.
